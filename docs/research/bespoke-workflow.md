# Individual body imagery

The user requested research-driven imagery for Solar System bodies, every moon and dwarf planet, plus the nearest 20 known exoplanets and 20 stars. Generic class maps stay as fallbacks. This is a continuing production queue, not a completed catalogue.

`bespoke-queue.json` contains 528 initial body tasks. JPL's live discovery table supplies 460 recognized planetary/Pluto satellites, including unnamed recognized objects; Earth's Moon brings this subset to 461. Dwarf-planet satellites outside the Pluto list are added for individual verification. The inventory of asteroid and other minor-planet satellites is still open. The current Solar System catalogue also contributes planets, the Sun and notable small bodies. Recognized dwarf planets are Ceres, Pluto, Eris, Haumea and Makemake; candidates stay explicitly labelled candidates.

`nearest-exoplanets.json` captures the current NASA composite archive. Each planet counts separately and ties at a host distance are broken by name. Retrieval fetched all rows inside 10 pc then sorted locally: the TAP service's initial TOP/order combination did not reliably yield the nearest objects. Composite values need individual publication checks; estimated radii must not become measured radii. `nearest-stars.json` uses the Gaia-era 10 pc sample v2 (Reylé and collaborators, 2023 update), counts stellar components including white dwarfs and excludes Sol/brown dwarfs. Its current boundary must be checked against newer astrometry; it is a preliminary working selection, not a claim that no closer object has since been found.

For each body:

1. Research primary measurements and the newest relevant papers. Record dates of observations separately from publication and retrieval. Identify disputed detections and contradictory measurements.
2. Assess native imagery. Point-source detection does not resolve a planet's surface or stellar photosphere. Preserve scientific maps and their actual coverage; distinguish natural colour, grayscale, infrared and enhanced colour. Never paint unobserved coverage into an observational map.
3. Write a dossier separating constraints, models, unknowns and chosen hypotheses. Unmeasured chemistry, atmosphere, cloud species and landforms remain imagined. Temperature/radius alone do not measure surface composition. Do not invent a solid ground on gas giants or stars: use explicitly labelled cloud-level or near-photosphere observer concepts.
4. Generate a unique surface map only when needed; a locality illustration can accompany an existing native map. Generate an observer/sky image with a stated viewpoint scenario. Use measured star radius, temperature and orbit to calculate stellar angular size, and assess parent-planet/companion visibility and illumination. Black skies are expected in effectively airless scenarios; dense atmospheres require a cited or explicit hypothetical scattering model. Native ground photographs take priority where available.
5. Record prompts/context, credits/terms, source file hashes, processing, uncertainties and palette choices. A generated image is not calibrated photometry: a temperature-based blackbody colour guide does not reproduce spectral lines, white balance, retinal adaptation or material reflectance. Verify star scale in the raster; retain limitations until calibrated. Do not claim a speculative landscape has “measured real composition.”
6. Install maps and companion images in `public/bespoke/`, register metadata in `src/data/bespoke.js`, update the body facts and research tracker, test/build and visually verify before committing. Maps are selected by full body name to avoid conflating every planet called b. Bespoke lunar/small-body maps replace existing analogs, while measured globe maps remain preferred. The preview embeds available companion images; its total size requires review as the library grows.

## First dossiers

Ceres retains the Dawn FC global mosaic. Its bespoke salt-terrain detail and observer view use dark material and pale salt crusts; exact metre-scale terrain is imagined. PIA24021 is a Dawn-derived perspective rendering with enhanced colour, not a true-colour ground photograph; it supplies terrain context only. The hypothetical sky uses a representative 2.77 AU distance, not the actual distance on a stated landing date. Raster Sun size remains an approximation despite a correction pass.

Barnard b is the 3.154-day planet discovered in 2024 and independently confirmed in 2025. The superseded 233-day 2018 candidate must not be used. Basant et al. supply minimum mass 0.299±0.026 Earth masses, 0.0229±0.0003 AU and a 438 K equilibrium model (zero albedo/full redistribution). Stellar values 3195 K and 0.185 solar radius give a calculated 4.306° stellar diameter. No measured planet radius, surface chemistry, atmosphere or terrain exists. The selected airless silicate/iron geology is one hypothesis, and the raster star colour/diameter is approximate. The ESO/Kornmesser reference is 2024 artwork, labelled IMAGINED.

`src/astro/appearance.js` computes disc size, central rectilinear width and bolometric illumination. Solar angular diameter is checked against recorded JPL Horizons observer values from Ceres on 2026-10-10/11. `scripts/assets/build-bespoke-queue.mjs` regenerates queue state from inventories and delivered dossiers; `scripts/assets/import-bespoke.mjs` installs these four generated inputs while preserving other dossiers.
